Understanding Flour Protein & Gluten Architecture

In bread making, "protein" refers primarily to two storage proteins found in wheat endosperm: gliadin and glutenin. When hydrated and agitated, these proteins bond via disulfide bridges to form gluten, the viscoelastic network that inflates with carbon dioxide gas during proofing and oven spring.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I add Vital Wheat Gluten to 100% rye bread? +
Yes. Rye contains mostly secalin rather than glutenin, which cannot form a traditional gluten matrix on its own. Adding 3% to 5% Vital Wheat Gluten to a high-percentage rye formula gives the dough sufficient structural elasticity to hold gas and prevent a gummy, heavy brick.
Does higher protein always mean better sourdough? +
Not necessarily. Excessively high protein (above 14.5%) can make dough stiff, rubbery, and overly elastic (resistant to stretching), leading to a tight, tough crumb rather than an open, tender, custardy crumb. For artisan country sourdough, 12.0% to 12.8% protein is considered the sweet spot.
