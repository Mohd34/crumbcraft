The Science of Sourdough Hydration: Why Recipe Hydration ≠ Total True Hydration

In traditional baking recipes, hydration is often loosely stated as the percentage of water poured into the mixing bowl relative to the dry flour added: (Water ÷ Flour) × 100%. However, when working with naturally fermented artisan sourdough, this simplified formula can lead to unpredictable, over-sticky doughs.

The Starter Contradiction

A standard sourdough starter is maintained at 100% hydration (equal parts water and flour by weight). When you add 200 grams of starter to your dough, you are not merely adding a leavening agent; you are introducing:

  • 100 grams of fermented flour
  • 100 grams of water

If your mixing bowl contains 1,000g of dry flour and you add 700g of water with 200g of 100% hydration starter:

  • Recipe Hydration: 700g ÷ 1,000g = 70.0%
  • Total True Hydration: (700g + 100g) ÷ (1,000g + 100g) = 800g ÷ 1,100g = 72.73%

That extra 2.73% water dramatically alters the dough's viscoelasticity, extensibility, and handling characteristics. If you use a stiff starter (such as a 50% hydration Italian lievito madre), the opposite occurs: the starter acts as a drying agent, reducing your total hydration from 70.0% down to 68.18%.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does ambient kitchen temperature affect dough hydration feel? +
Warm temperatures accelerate enzymatic activity (specifically amylase and protease). At temperatures above 78°F (25.5°C), protease enzymes break down gluten bonds faster, causing the dough to feel slack, wet, and sticky, even if the hydration percentage has not changed. Using our Desired Dough Temperature (DDT) Calculator helps keep your dough at the ideal 75°F–78°F window.
Can whole wheat flour absorb more water than white bread flour? +
Yes! Whole wheat and whole rye flours contain wheat bran and germ, along with pentosans (hemicellulose polymers) that can absorb up to 10 to 15 times their dry weight in water. When blending 20% to 30% whole wheat into your recipe, you can typically increase your recipe hydration by 3% to 5% without the dough becoming slack.
Why is salt always calculated at 2% in baker's math? +
Salt provides three essential functions in artisan bread: flavor enhancement, yeast regulation, and gluten tightening. Salt ions mask electrical charges on gluten proteins, allowing them to bind more tightly and produce a stronger gluten matrix. Most artisan recipes use between 1.8% and 2.2% fine sea salt.
