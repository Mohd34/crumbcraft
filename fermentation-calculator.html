🎯 Why Target Rise Changes With Temperature

At 24.0°C (75.2°F), your dough will retain moderate heat when placed in the fridge, taking 3–4 hours to cool. Stop bulk fermentation when the dough has risen 50%–60% to allow for carry-over fermentation in the refrigerator.

  • Dough Visuals: Domed surface, aerated edges, marshmallow feel.
  • Shake Test: Jiggles noticeably when bowl is shaken gently.
  • Gas Development: Visible bubbles under the surface skin.

Underproofed vs Overproofed Crumb Diagnostic

Diagnostic Feature Underproofed Loaf Perfect Open Crumb Overproofed Loaf
Crumb Texture Dense, gummy baseline with gigantic tunneling cavern holes near top crust ("fool's crumb"). Evenly distributed, custardy, open alveoli throughout entire loaf. Tear-away ragged holes, pale crust, flat dense crumb, flying top crust.
Oven Spring Explosive or erratic bursting along score line or tearing at base. Tall, rounded cross-section with pronounced ear and blooming score. Flat saucer pancake shape; scores melt and fail to bloom in oven.
Flavor Bland, yeasty, lacking complex lactic/acetic balance. Complex, sweet toasted grain aroma with balanced gentle lactic tang. Sharply sour, vinegar-like acidity; unpleasant astringent aftertaste.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an aliquot jar in sourdough baking? +
An aliquot jar is a small, straight-sided cylindrical container (like a spice jar or shot glass) where you place a small sample (e.g. 30g–40g) of your freshly mixed dough. By marking the starting line with a rubber band, you can accurately track the exact percentage volume rise (e.g. 50% or 75%) without disturbing the main dough bowl during coil folds.
When does bulk fermentation start and finish? +
Bulk fermentation begins the exact moment active starter (levain) is mixed into the flour and water. It ends when you divide and shape your loaves into bannetons. Stretch and folds occur during the first 2 hours of bulk fermentation, not before it.
