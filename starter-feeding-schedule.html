🕒 Fermentation Milestones

  • Hours 1–2 (Lag Phase): Yeast acclimating, minimal visible rise.
  • Hours 3–5 (Log Phase): Rapid bubbling, dome rising, volume doubles.
  • Peak Window: 6.8 hours after feeding (Dome convex, max gas)
  • Post-Peak (Collapse): Dome flattens, gluten weakens, acidity increases.

Sourdough Starter Microbiology: Inoculation Ratios Explained

The ratio at which you feed your sourdough starter is one of the most powerful control knobs in artisan bread making. It determines not only when your starter will be ready to bake, but also the flavor profile (acetic acid vinegar vs lactic acid yogurt) and proteolytic enzymatic degradation of your dough.

Frequently Asked Questions

What flour blend is best for feeding sourdough starter? +
A 50/50 blend of unbleached bread flour and whole rye flour (or whole wheat) is widely considered the gold standard. Rye contains high levels of fermentable sugars (fructose and amylase) and wild microbes that stimulate vigorous fermentation, while white bread flour provides gluten structure that traps gas and enables tall rise in your jar.
Can I bake with my starter straight from the fridge? +
If your starter was fed right before going into the fridge and is less than 3–4 days old, you can use cold discard in pancakes or crackers, but for artisan sourdough loaves, you should always give it at least one active room-temperature feeding to ensure the yeast and lactic acid bacteria have recovered from dormancy.
